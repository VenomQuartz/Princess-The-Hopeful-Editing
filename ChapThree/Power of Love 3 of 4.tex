\subsubsection*{The Finest Hour, the Last Hour (Fight \textbullet\textbullet\textbullet\textbullet\textbullet, Tempesta \textbullet\textbullet\textbullet\textbullet)}

\noindent \textbf{Action:} Instant, Resolve + Composure

\noindent \textbf{Cost:} 3 Wisps, 1 Willpower, 1 Resistant Aggravated Damage

\noindent \textbf{Duration:} 1 scene \\

\noindent The Princess draws upon the dedication and resolve the Seraphic General displayed during the fall, and recreates her own lesser version of that courage to become Storms' avatar. 

    \begin{myindentpar}{10pt}\emph{Dramatic Failure:} The Princess is overcome with a feeling of utter futility and defeatism. She loses all Willpower and must flee all combat for the scene. She is considered to have broken Tempesta's ban.

    \emph{Failure:} The Princess cannot focus.

    \emph{Success:} The Princess enters a combat trance, a tranquil fury, or even a screaming hysteria. In this state she is one of the most powerful and terrible combatants known to the Radiant. She gains the Seraphic Fury Tilt: Any damage she inflicts is Aggravated, she gains temporary health equal to her Inner Light + Resolve and every time she kills someone she believes to be in league with either the Darkness or the Court of Tears she completely refreshes her Willpower and gains a single box of temporary Health. There is no upper limit to her maximum health, in extreme cases a Fury could continue fighting long after her body has been utterly destroyed (she would resemble an unusually solid Goalenu at this point). 

    \emph{Exceptional Success:} The Princess develops such as strong aura of violence and power that her foes flee at the very sight of her. She also gains the Terrifying tilt, the people affected are Mortals (including minor templates like Sworn and Darkened) and Darkspawn.\end{myindentpar}

\textbf{Drawback:} Once this Charm is active the Princess must fight to the death - she simply cannot conceive of not fighting for as long as one foe remains standing. The Princess also gains the Invocation Lock condition keyed to Tempesta.

\subsubsection*{Consecrate (Govern \textbullet)}

\noindent \textbf{Action:} Extended, Presence + Expression (10 minutes/roll, threshold = variable)

\noindent \textbf{Cost:} 3 Wisps

\noindent \textbf{Duration:} special\\

\noindent The Princess blesses the ground she stands on, and dedicates it temporarily to her Queen and the Light. By using this Charm a Princess applies the Consecrated condition to the area until the Charm ends. Many Charms, distinguished as such in their description are more durable or effective when invoked upon consecrated groups. The Princess needs to cleanse and purify every part of the area she wishes to sanctify; this can be as simple as giving it a thorough spring cleaning, or it can be an elaborate rite for casting out evil spirits, depending on the Princess' cultural background. However, if she applies an Invocation, the method of purification invokes the relevant Queen and must use symbols appropriate for her. 

Consecrating a large area is naturally harder than a small one, Consecrate is a Sanctuary based Charm. 

    \begin{myindentpar}{10pt}\emph{Dramatic Failure:} The Princess ruins the area's blessing - all her accumulated successes are lost, and if the area has the Consecrated  condition immediately loses it. No one can use Consecrate there for a full month.

    \emph{Failure:} The Princess makes no progress, but may continue her efforts.

    \emph{Success:} The Princess progresses in her work. When she reaches the threshold, the area gains the Consecrated condition and retains it for 1 day. The Princess may continue working to extend the Charm's duration:

    2 * Sanctuary Size = 1 day
    
    4 * Sanctuary Size = 1 week
    
    6 * Sanctuary Size = 1 month
    
    8 * Sanctuary Size = 1 year
    
    10 * Sanctuary Size = indefinite 

    \emph{Exceptional Success:} The Princess makes great progress.\end{myindentpar}

\noindent  If an area is already Consecrated, Consecrate will not take effect until the Princess reaches enough successes to exceed the remaining duration of the previous Consecration. (For instance, if an area will be Consecrated for five days, the Princess must reach enough successes for a week's duration, after which the area will be Consecrated for a week.) If any part of an area is Tainted, the Charm automatically fails. Consecrate ends if the area suffers major alterations or damage, or someone manages to bring Taint into it.

If the Princess has access to gems created by the Charm Enduring Beauty she can use them to empower Consecrated ground, extending the duration of the protection. A gem worth Resources three increases the duration by one step (so 2 * the area's size successes creates a duration of 1 week). A gem worth Resources five increases the duration by two steps. It takes five gems worth Resources one to increase the duration by one step. Multiple gems may be combined for a greater effect but the required successes can can never drop below 2 * the area's size. Naturally the duration cannot be increased past indefinite. 

Gems used to empower Consecrated Ground must be reworked into part of the ground, becoming beautiful statues, stained glass windows or other features. This happens as part of the Charm. They must be displayed prominently but can be protected by any anti-theft measure that doesn’t spoil their beauty. If even one of these reworked gems is removed from the Consecrated ground the Charm ends. Once the gems are transformed happens they can never again be used to enhance magic, however they are still beautiful and may be worth money as works of art, and they can still be used to return beauty to whomever it was taken from.

Making use of Consecrated ground that is built on stolen beauty is a Belief Compromise, unless there is good evidence to suggest that the beauty was taken consensually. There is no modifier for the Compromise roll.

\begin{myindentpar}{10pt} \textbf{Upgrade: Purifying}

The Princess may apply the Consecrated condition on top of Taint. The Taint will always overcome this Charm given enough time, but it can allow the Princess some vital breathing space. Invoke Consecrate like normal, but take the Taint’s Severity as a penalty to your dice pool, then:

   \begin{itemize} \item Outside of its Nightscape, the Taints’ Aspirations go dormant. 
    \item Outside of its Nightscape, the Taint will not attempt to Corrupt, nor will it affect dice pools with its Severity.
    \item The Taint no longer provides access to and from its Nightscape, the Dark Door is sealed shut and the Enter Darkness ability fails.
	\item The Taint remains vulnerable to any technique that might purify it, including Cleansing, if anyone happens to be inside of its Nightscape when it is closed off they remain trapped in the Dark World. \end{itemize}

However once per day reduce the Princesses Successes by the Taint’s Severity. When her Successes are reduced to zero or when the duration expires, as measured by her unmodified Successes, the Consecrated condition ends and the normal effects of Taint return. The Princess may invoke Purify again before its duration ends, replacing the existing instance of Purify with the new one upon completion (she still takes a dice pool penalty equal to the Severity of the Tainted Place).

Purifying only protects against the existing Taint, any new Taint generated in the area will end the effects.

\textbf{Upgrade: Bastion}

Several charms have special benefits when cast upon Consecrated ground. When Consecrate is invoked with Bastion another benefit is added: Charms that benefit from the Consecrated condition are immune to being dispelled (such as by the Charm Unweaving) for as long as the condition remains.

It is important to note that Consecrate itself can be dispelled and this Upgrade offers no protection against that.

\end{myindentpar}

\subsubsection*{Reflected Light (Govern \textbullet)}

\noindent \textbf{Action:} Permanent\\ 

\noindent The Princess gains the benefits of the Unseen Sense merit for all supernatural phenomena within 10 x Inner Light yards. Any being with a Rank or Potency trait (including creatures of the Darkness, as they use Shadows as a pusdo-Potency) will trigger her sense upon stepping within Inner Light x 10 yards of the Princess, and any active use of their powers will trigger her sense again. A Mortal with Supernatural Merits or a template that doesn't grant Potency (such as Sworn) will not trigger her sense, but active use of their abilities will do so. 

The Princess may roll Wits + Empathy reflexively when her sense is triggered, and may apply an Invocation to this roll. If she sensed something of the Light or the Darkness, success identifies the power as of the Light or the Darkness. For other powers the Princess must learn about the nature of the power through research or investigation. So if a Princess felt a power belonging to a Vampire a success will inform her that it was the same kind of power as every other use of a Vampire's power she previously felt. But she would not know that all these powers belonged to Vampires without old fashioned investigating. 

If the character using the power has the ability to conceal itself from supernatural detection, then the Princess must overcome that concealment as per the usual rules.  

\begin{myindentpar}{10pt} \textbf{Upgrade: Sensitive}

Double the range of the Princess' Unseen Sense, to 20 x Inner Light yards.

\textbf{Upgrade: Light Attuned}

The Princess recognizes individual powers springing directly from the Light; when she rolls to identify a Charm, she learns the exact Charm along with any Upgrades and which Invocation, if any, was applied. 

\textbf{Upgrade: Dark Attuned (Tempesta \textbullet)}

The Princess recognizes individual powers springing directly from the Darkness; when she rolls to identify an Umbra or Caligo, she learns the specific power, not just \textquotedblleft power from the Darkness". If the Storyteller rules that other supernatural entities draw powers from the Darkness; the Princess can identify any such powers.\end{myindentpar}


\subsubsection*{Dreaming Sight (Govern \textbullet)}

\noindent \textbf{Action:} Reflexive\\ 

\noindent \textbf{Cost:} 1 Wisp\\ 

\noindent \textbf{dice pool:} Unrolled\\ 

\noindent \textbf{Duration:} 1 Scene\\ 

\noindent The Princess can see into Twilight to perceive natives of the Dreamlands who have escaped to the waking world, along with similar phenomena like astrally projecting psychics. With a Successful Perception roll she can notice Manifestation Conditions related to a Dreamlanders in her vicinity (If playing a crossover with Mage: the Awakened, remember that native Dreamlander is the Nobility's term for Goetica). Physical objects still block her sight, so a Dreamlander can hide behind - or inside - such objects to evade her.	

\begin{myindentpar}{10pt} \textbf{Upgrade: Granted}

By touching the eyes of another the Princess may bestow the benefits of this Charm upon them with an Instant Action. 

\textbf{Upgrade: Touching}
 
By spending an additional Wisp, the Princess shifts her flesh partly into Twilight, allowing her to touch or attack Dreamlanders, and they her, until the Charm ends. All of her regalia (including weapons and armor) are carried into this state with her, as are the effects of Charms. If the Princess has Spirit Sight or Second Sight she may choose which types of ephemeral beings (along with their related phenomina) to apply Touching too when she activates the Charm. 

\textbf{Upgrade: Spirit Sight (Legno \textbullet)}
One of the Wilds may attune herself to the presence of spirits. The Princess also sees spirits in Twilight Form, and can make Perception rolls to notice Manifestation Conditions related to a spirit, or the presence of a locus [GMC 220] and related phenomena.

\textbf{Upgrade: Second Sight (Lacrima \textbullet)}

A follower of Tears can part the veil and see into the great beyond. The Princess also sees ghosts in Twilight Form and structures made of ghostly ephemera, and can make Perception rolls to notice Manifestation Conditions related to a ghost, a ghost’s Anchor or an Avernian Gate [GMC 226] and related phenomena.\end{myindentpar}

\begin{mybox}

\noindent\textbf{What About Angels?} \\

\noindent Readers may wonder why there isn’t an upgrade to perceive the third type of ephemeral beings described in the Chronicles of Darkness. We left it out, along with any other explicit reference to the God Machine, because in our opinion it’s not possible to add the God-Machine to a game of Princess: the Hopeful without turning it into the centrepiece of the story. A campaign in which a nakama discovers the God Machine and tries to do something about it could be an excellent thing, but we would need much more than a few Charms to do justice to it.\end{mybox}


\subsubsection*{Study Inheritance (Govern \textbullet)}

\noindent \textbf{Action:} Extended, Intelligence + Empathy (5 minutes/roll, threshold = Bequest's Merit rating)

The mysteries of the Kingdom's relics can be unveiled. The Princess uses this Charm on a transformed Bequest; when she reaches the threshold, she discovers what Charm the Bequest is based on, the cost of activating it, whether it carries a pool of Wisps, if it is empowered, and the duty one must carry out to refill the pool.

\subsubsection*{Charge (Govern \textbullet\textbullet)}

\noindent \textbf{Action:} Instant, unrolled

\noindent \textbf{Cost:} none

\noindent \textbf{Duration:} Lasting \\

\noindent The Princess may transfer some of her own Wisps to to another. She and the target must be physically near to each other, as a rule of thumb they need to be within shouting distance, and the Princess must be able to create an emotional reaction, no matter how small. Once both conditions are satisfied the Princess wills her magic to travel and it does so, in a glowing, sparkling bolt of energy, filling the target's emotional reaction with magical power. 

The Princess can also transfer Wisps into an object, providing it has a Wisp Pool. This is harder, and requires the Princess to touch the target object. 

Transferring a Wisp through Charge counts as spending a Wisp when measuring against a Princess' Wisp per turn limit, and she cannot give more Wisps than the target can hold.

\begin{myindentpar}{10pt} \textbf{Upgrade: Accepted}

The Princess may take Wisps from other people, if they are willing to give them. If the target consents, the Princess may use this charm in reverse to draw wisps into herself. Inanimate objects like Bequests are obviously unable to give consent to anything. 

\textbf{Upgrade: Distant}

\textbf{Cost: 1 Wisp}

\noindent The Princess may use the Charm on people who are not present. She spends a Wisp and rolls Presence + Empathy - Intimacy, and may apply an Invocation to the roll. If she succeeds she creates a magical link through which she can transfer Wisps, this link lasts for a scene. If the Princess has Accepted, she can take Wisps through this link as well. 

\textbf{Upgrade: Sweet Dreams}

The Princess can convert between Wisps and Astral Essence at a 1:1 ratio, in either direction, she may also use Charge and all its Upgrades on a Dramlander. Princesses cannot store Essence, she must convert Wisps into Essence before offering it to a Dramlander, and transform any Essence received into Wisps.

As there are an awful lot of Dreamlanders with Essence pools and loyalty to a Queen and her Court the Storyteller should be careful not to let the Dreamlands become an infinite source of free Wisps. Running low on Wisps is one of the ways Princess: the Hopeful can give mechanical heft to the Mood of Exhaustion. But at the same time, when it's dramatically appropriate one good night's sleep recharging the player characters can be a big part of portraying exhaustion. Use your judgement and everything should be fine. 

\textbf{Upgrade: Stolen (Lacrima \textbullet\textbullet)}

\noindent \textbf{Prerequisite:} Accepted

\noindent \textbf{Cost:} Compromise at Belief 4+ with a -3 modifer

The Princess can take Wisps from the Light-Touched, even without consent. She must roll Presence + Intimidation - Composure and cannot Transfer more Wisps per turn than her Successes. Stolen cannot be combined with Distant. 

A Raven can also steal from a Beacon, even though Beacons do not have Wisps their Light is fragile and can be stolen away entirely. Any Beacon who is successfully drained takes the Running on Fumes Condition until they next regain some Willpower. If a Beacon is drained too much they can permanently loose the template and develop a Persistent condition such as Depression to represent the consequences of having their optimism stolen. A Breaking Point is probably appropriate. Draining a Beacon dry can shake a Princess' Belief harder than the Lacrima backlash for this Upgrade, but as always a single action can only cause one Beleif Compromise. 

The Storyteller has discretion over how much a Beacon can be drained without permanent effects, but as a rule of thumb they can recover from loosing around  half (Integrity + Willpower) wisps. After a month without being targeted any previous thefts no longer apply. 

\textbf{Upgrade: Capitation (Lacrima \textbullet\textbullet\textbullet)}

\noindent \textbf{Prerequisite:} Accepted

\noindent \textbf{Cost:} Compromise at Belief 4+ with a -3 modifer

Alhambra is the one true nation for all humanity, and so Alhambra's officials have the duty to take the city's due from all of humanity. The Noble can extract hope and joy from a
mundane human, converting them into Wisps for herself. After at least fifteen minutes of uninterrupted conversation, during which she touches her target at least once, she rolls Resolve + Intimidation - target’s Composure. The Charm may not be used on anyone more than once a scene and may not be combined with Distant.

\emph{Dramatic Failure:} The target becomes deeply disturbed by the Noble. She takes the Notoriety Condition (CofD page 290) related to him.\\
\emph{Failure:} The Noble gains nothing from the target.\\
\emph{Success:} The Noble taxes the target’s hope. She gains one Wisp per success and the target takes the Depression Condition for a number of days per success. Until the Condition is resolved the target cannot be drained by Capitation.\\ 
\emph{Exceptional Success:} As for normal success, and the target becomes captivated by the Noble, and wants to talk to her again. He gains the Spooked Condition (CofD page 291) linked to the Noble.

\textbf{Upgrade: In Memoriam (Lacrima \textbullet\textbullet)}

The Princess can convert between Wisps and Ghostly Essence at a 2:1 ratio, in either direction, she may also use Charge and all its Upgrades on a Ghost. Princesses cannot store Essence - she must converted Wisps into Essence before offering it to a ghost, and transform any Essence received into Wisps.

\textbf{Upgrade: Significance (Legno \textbullet\textbullet)}

The Princess can convert between Wisps and Spiritual Essence at a 2:1 ratio, in either direction, she may also use Charge and all its Upgrades on a Spirit. Princesses cannot store Essence - she must converted Wisps into Essence before offering it to a Spirit, and transform any Essence received into Wisps.\end{myindentpar}

\noindent \textbf{Cost:} 1 Wisp

\noindent Invoking the Light's authority, the Princess drives off a being of the Darkness. If the Princess wins the contest, the target becomes irrationally afraid of her for the duration; gaining the Terrified Tilt.

\begin{myindentpar}{10pt}

Inhuman monsters must acknowledge the Princess' authority. She may use the Charm on non-human supernatural beings, such as ghosts and spirits.

\textbf{Upgrade: Majestic}

All those with power bow to the Light. The Princess may use the Charm on humans with supernatural powers that aren't derived from the Light. This includes anyone with a major or minor template, and anyone with powers they would lose if they gained a template, except for Beacons, Sworn and Hopeful.

\subsubsection*{Light over All (Govern \textbullet\textbullet)}

\noindent \textbf{Action:} Permanent \\

\noindent Mortals may not be magical, but they can use magic. Some forms of magic used by mortals are impossible for a Princess to perform, others are rendered obsolete, and then there are some types of magic that Princesses find come even easier after they Blossom. 

On the whole Princesses aren't that great at working with or against Ephemeral Beings (Dreamlanders excepted). The noble magic that affects such beings tends to be basic, with only obscure Embassies mastering anything substantial. Ghosts are easier to work with but to learn Charms that affect Ghosts she would have to first become a practitioner of Lacrima, even though Lacrima's Ghost Charms do not compromise Belief few among the Radiant are willing to take that step.

So if Princesses want to deal with an Ephemeral Being they'll either call the an Embassy, or they'll turn to the same tricks that mortals use (see GMC p230). It is fortunate then, that a Princess' own magic can reinforce mortal techniques, with Light over All she can apply an Invocation to the rolls. 

In addition, the following dice bonuses and penalties apply, based on the entity:

\begin{itemize} \item    +3: The entity was never human, and is anathema to humanity (Some creatures of the Darkness)
\item    +1: The entity was never human, but is not anathema to humanity; or was human, but thinks nothing like a human (most Spirits and Dreamlanders, some ancient Ghosts)
\item    -1: The entity was once human, and still thinks in somewhat human ways (Ghosts, some Dreamlanders)
\end{itemize}

\begin{myindentpar}{10pt}\textbf{Upgrade: Blood Magic (Tempesta \textbullet)}

During any roll where Light over All applies, the Princess may take a point of resistant bashing damage to add +3 to one roll.\end{myindentpar}


\subsubsection*{Seed of Light (Govern \textbullet\textbullet)}

\noindent \textbf{Action:} Extended, Dexterity + Expression ((20 - Willpower) minutes/roll, threshold = 3 * seeds made)

\noindent \textbf{Cost:} 1 Willpower, 1+ Wisps (special)\\

\noindent The Light exists within the world, within the hearts of all men. However, with will and effort, the Hopeful may calcify Wisps into gem-sized, imperishable Seeds, proof against the ravages of chance. Each Wisp spent on the Charm forms a separate Seed. The Princess does not have to decide how many Seeds to calcify beforehand; after spending the Willpower, she starts to concentrate on her pool of Wisps. Each three successes accumulated allows the Princess to transform one Wisp into a Seed, and she may roll a number of times equal to her dice pool, or until she runs out of Wisps. Extra successes are wasted

The shape of a Seed is a profound expression of its creator's soul, and is heavily influenced by the Invocation (if any) applied in its making. (Applying Legno, for instance, often creates Seeds in the form of living, unchanging flora.) Any character examining a Seed, if they make a Wits + Occult roll, can recognize a Seed as coming from the same hand as one they've seen before (Invocations are usually clear without a roll). 

Any character with a Wisp pool can recover the Wisp from a Seed, to do so they must spend a minute in concentration. No actions may be performed while doing this, and the character is at -3 to all Wits-based rolls, so deep is their focus. The Seed glows, and becomes incohesive, melting into their soul, and adding the Wisp to their pool. If the character's Wisp pool is full, the Seed vaporizes and releases the Wisp into the air, where it dissipates.

As tiny fragments of solidified Light, Seeds are incredibly alluring to creatures of the Darkness. Any character with the Darkened template can consume Seeds to regain Willpower, gaining one point of Willpower per Seed eaten. The Darkened can smell out Seeds kept in quantity - they gain a cumulative +1 bonus to all Perception rolls to notice a person carrying Seeds for each Seed above the carrier's Composure, and tend to aim first at opponents carrying the most Seeds. Moreover, any location where large numbers of Seeds are stored can be noticed for miles. For this reason, Alhambran outposts tend to either be very heavily protected, or to try to move their ill gotten gains back to their rain-soaked city as fast as possible. And for the same reason, servants of the Queen of Storms have been known to use captured Seeds as Darkspawn bait.

\begin{myindentpar}{10pt}\textbf{Upgrade: Bereaved (Lacrima \textbullet\textbullet\textbullet)}

The production of Seeds by Alhambrans occurs on an industrial scale. Reduce the time per roll to once per turn. 

As a side effect of applying this Upgrade, the character removes the unique traces of their identity from the Seeds they make. Instead, the Seed forms as one of the seals of the Kingdom, the crown of the Queen of Tears prominent on it. The production of such a Seed is frequently used as a test of identity by an Alhambran outpost sent a new Noble, and documents and packages sent between groups are sealed with wax imprinted with this mark. This leaves unmistakable magical traces, which a character with Lacrima 1 or higher, or the ability to see auras, can detect with a Wits + Occult roll, making forging the seal harder.

\textbf{Upgrade: Militant (Tempesta \textbullet\textbullet or Fuoco \textbullet\textbullet\textbullet)}

One cannot simply wait for the Light to diffuse into one's soul! All of existence is a war against the Darkness, and no soldier of the Seraphic General should be caught without her arms! When creating the Seed, the Noble may choose to make it release its energy quickly. If Tempesta was applied, the Wisp can be recovered from the Seed as a reflexive action, at a cost of 1 resistant Bashing  damage, with no more than skin contact with the Seed. There is no limit to how many Seeds can be tapped in this way per round, beyond the limits of one's well-being.

The followers of the Queen of Swords are less rash, but no less aware of the dangers of the world. By applying Fuoco, they create Seeds that release their Wisp as an instant action.\end{myindentpar}

\subsubsection*{Safety in Mortality (Govern \textbullet\textbullet)}

\noindent \textbf{Action:} Full turn, Resolve + Intimidation

\noindent \textbf{Cost:} 1 Wisp

\noindent \textbf{Duration:} 1 scene, (Consecrated) \\

\noindent A Princess is as much a being of mortality as she is of the Light. Just as the Transformation allows her to switch between these worlds she may push these changes outwards, creating a safe space through which magic cannot enter. The Charm is used on an enclosed space, such as a building or a fenced park - if the Princess needs to protect an area that isn't enclosed, she must mark its border somehow, by (for instance) drawing a chalk line or throwing down a rope. The area must not extend more than (Inner Light x 20) yards in any direction, unless the area is Consecrated.

\begin{myindentpar}{10pt}    \emph{Dramatic Failure:} The Princess makes herself vulnerable to others' magic. Any supernatural power that is normally resisted, when used on her, gets a +1 bonus on its activation roll for 1 day.

    \emph{Failure:} The area gains no protection.

    \emph{Success:} The Princess applies the Warded condition to the target area for one scene; any attempt to direct magic from outside to inside the protected space triggers a Clash of Wills against the Princess' Clash of Wills dice pool at the point this Charm was invoked, and the Princess is aware of any such attempts. If this Charm was invoked as a teamwork action the Primary Actor's Clash of Wills dice pool is used, but all participants are aware of any attempts to breach the Wards. If the enclosed space has the Consecrated Condition, the Charm lasts until the area is no longer Consecrated and the Warded Condition extends to cover the complete Consecrated are.

    \emph{Exceptional Success:} The Charm's protection lasts for a full day.\end{myindentpar}


\subsubsection*{Accept Fealty (Govern \textbullet\textbullet, Invocation \textbullet\textbullet\textbullet)}

\noindent \textbf{Action:} Extended, Presence + Persuasion - Intimacy (10 minutes/roll, threshold = target's Willpower)

\noindent \textbf{Cost:} 1 Wisp/roll, 1 Willpower dot.

\noindent \textbf{Duration:} Lasting \\

\noindent Through a Ceremonial induction the Princess can invite mortals to join her Court, becoming Sworn. The ceremony is typically held in a relatively quiet and isolated location. This is for pragmatic reasons, as the process of the Charm creates a rather impressive light show. For the Sworn, the ceremony is an enlightening experience, no pun intended. They see the image of the Queen they have chosen to serve, for a moment they share in everything the Queen means to the Princess accepting their fealty, and in that moment they share in the Princess' magic. When the ceremony ends a small spark of Light remains.

The target must understand what he is doing, and do it voluntarily; on an unknowing or unwilling target, the Charm fails. It also fails on mortals steeped in villainy; the target must not be Darkened or be a supernatural being (the exact line is fuzzy and up to the Storyteller, but anything with a Potency trait is out). Each Queen has a different version of this Charm, and a Princess can learn or use only the one for her own Queen.

Note that while Accept Fealty subtracts Intimacy from its dice pool it cannot be performed at a distance, and penalty is not reduced to -0 for having line of sight. Many Princesses will gladly swear a mortal into their Court as a favor to Princesses who don't know this Charm or belong to another Court less suitable for the Sworn-to-be, in exchange for a Bequest or a favor worth a dot of Willpower. They power through the Intimacy penalty through high attributes. 

\begin{myindentpar}{10pt}\textbf{Upgrade: Light's Best Friend}

The Princess can accept an animal companion into her service. The animal must give informed consent which usually takes the Beast Speaker charm, though some species such as dogs are naturally good with humans and can figure it out. They gain the same practical magic as a human, which may or may not be useful for their species. And like any sworn they may use Bequests, to the limit of their species' abilities. Though some care may need to be taken to place a Bequest into a usable form. A dog could wear a scarf that bestows Celestial Grace, but would struggle to use a pair of magical shoes tied to its back.

\end{myindentpar}


\subsubsection*{Light's Riposte (Govern \textbullet\textbullet, Fuoco \textbullet)}

\noindent \textbf{Cost:} 1 Wisp

\noindent \textbf{Duration:} instant\\

\noindent Those who cast hostile magics at the Princess shall suffer for their temerity. When she is the target of a supernatural effect that she recognizes as such, she may activate the Charm. For each success rolled, the magic's user takes 1 bashing damage. This may be done only once per effect. 

\begin{myindentpar}{10pt}\textbf{Upgrade: Lethal (Fuoco \textbullet\textbullet)}

\noindent \textbf{Cost:} +1 Wisp

The Princess's rebuke grows harsher; for each success rolled, the target takes 1 lethal damage.\end{myindentpar}

\subsubsection*{A Magical Girl's Eyes (Govern \textbullet\textbullet\textbullet)}

\noindent \textbf{Requires:} Reflected Light\\

\noindent \textbf{Cost:} 1 Wisp\\

\noindent \textbf{Action:} Reflexive, Unrolled\\

\noindent \textbf{Duration:} 1 Scene\\

\noindent In her Transformed identity a Princess is an inherently magical being. Her shining heart is comprised of both muscle tissue and solid magic, her fingers are made of flesh and clever little enchantments, her eyes, those are magic too. By spending a Wisp the Princess can see (or smell, or taste or somehow sense) magic. This includes both magical beings, magical items or places, and the presence of lasting magical enchantments. This sense extends as far as she can see with her eyes and can be enhanced with binoculars, a telescope or remote vision Charms, but not over a television or camera.

By rolling Intelligence + Empathy (for a Princess's magic), Science (for a mad scientist's work), or Occult (for anything else) the Princess can also determine what kind of magic she is looking at. She may apply an Invocation to this roll. However the Princess cannot see in any detail. At best she might get something like \textquotedblleft it looks angry" when looking at a werewolf or a werewolf's spell. If the Princess sees the same kind of magic again she can recognize the similarity; to the normal limits of her memory. If the Princess learns that the angry looking person is a werewolf she can conclude that all the other people or enchantments with the same angry look are also werewolves or their creations.

If she is looking at an active magic effect or magical item she can determine its purpose to a similar level of detail, such as \textquotedblleft protect this building". If the Princess has the Reflected Light upgrades Light Attuned or Dark Attuned she gains their benefits when using Magical Girl's Eyes, if she has Study Inheritance then she only needs to look at a Bequest to learn it's particulars. As with all uses of vision this power is less effective over distance, as a rule of thumb the Princess's ability to analyze magic fades with distance at the same rate as her ability to recognize faces.

This Charm provides no ability to see into Twilight, though if the Princess has some other means of seeing into Twilight it will apply to it's full extent upon the residents of that plane. If the enchantment itself is constructed to be hidden from poking and prying, or if the enchantment conceals things such as by making them invisible the Princess may roll a Clash of Wills to overcome such deceptions.

\subsubsection*{Items of Power}

When a Princess quests through the dreamlands for the Bequest's form what exactly is she looking for? 

The answer is just about anything suitable. Lets say the Princess wishes to create a Levinbolt Bequest, to do so she'd need to find a powerful ranged weapon in her dreams. 

If she knows Levinbolt she could search through her own Crawlspace for her Levinbolt Regalia and use that (a different Charm that does not create Regalia will still have a physical form symbolising the Princess' mastery of the Charm in Crawlspace). This will enable her to create a Bequest that has the same, or some of the Upgrades, that she already knows. 

Or she could look for a suitable weapon in the Dreamlands, and she can find one anywhere where it is sensible for her to do so. The Queens' domains are sure to have equipment that can power the more basic charms, and as Nobility she would have little trouble asking for some. Out in the wider Dreamlands she could for example, go to Starfleet (the Dreamlands city reflecting the Star Trek franchise) and take a phaser from any red shirt or principle character. However despite phasers being highly advanced futuristic weapons only the most die-hard fan places much mental or emotional significance on a phaser and thus acquiring one would only create a weak Levinbolt Bequest with no Upgrades. If instead the Princess acquired Robin Hood's bow and arrow (a difficult task given that Robin Hood is a skilled outlaw even before taking into account the Rank fame bestows upon his Dreamlands reflection) she could create a mighty bequest, for Robin Hood's archery is a core part of a very well known legend. 

Robin Hood's bow has another advantage: A Bequest's Transformed from resembles the Dreamlands object it is made from, and making a Bequest that looks like a bow and arrow in it's transformed state and is less overtly supernatural than one that looks like a phaser. At least until mortal science creates directed energy weaponry.

The Tainted Aspirations reflect the flaws of Lacrima, and like all Tainted Aspirations they require inflicting cruelty. \subsubsection*{Unweaving (Govern \textbullet\textbullet\textbullet\textbullet)}

\noindent \textbf{Action:} Reactive, Instant, or Extended

\noindent \textbf{Dice Pool:} Clash of Wills or Dexterity + Empathy (5 minutes/roll, Threshold = Special )

\noindent Cost 2 Wisps\\

To unravel magical defenses, to unpick a curse upon a beloved little sister, to tear apart a hostile Charm flying at you. There are many reasons why a Princess might need to unweave the Charms and magic of another. 

Unweaving can be thought of as two Charms in one, both work on the same underlying principle: The charm allows the Princess to interact with magic as though it were a physical thing, and if she can tough magic she can destroy it.

The first effect allows the Princess to trigger a Clash of Wills against any supernatural effect. 

If invoked reactively the Princess grabs hold of the unfinished magic and tries to rip it apart while her opponent tries to hold the magic together. In short, Unweaving lets the Princess trigger a Clash of Wills at any time her opponent casts a spell on a target within arms reach.

The second effect allows the Princess to end a magical effect that is already active. An enchantment that has been completed and taken hold is tougher to break: Instead of a Contested Action the Princess must score more Successes than her opponent's Clash of Wills dice pool. She can attempt to grab the magic magic and rip it apart as an Instant action, or she can delicately unweave it thread by thread as an Extended action. 

Unweaving can only be used against \textquotedblleft magic power\textquotedblright\, abilities like Charms. Innate abilities such as Transformation or Sensitivity are unaffected. The Princess also needs to be able to touch the magic, usually this means being within the affected area or within arms reach of the target. 

\begin{mybox}

\noindent \textbf{Disciplines, Gifts and Contracts. Oh My!}\\

\noindent Can a Princess unravel a Gift, or unpick a Contract? Well no. By default a Princess can only use Unweaving on magic created by Princesses, the Darkness and other things found in the Princess books. Even when a Gift is mechanically similar to a Charm they are metaphysically different enough that a Princess cannot unpick the threads and end the magic.

This is true, unless you, the Storyteller, decides it is not. Allowing Princesses to undo the magic of other's is certainly an advantage over other creatures, but if all the players are Princesses or the other creatures are given an equivalent ability to undo Charms this might not be a problem. You might choose to allow Unweaving to work against all forms of magic. Against foreign magic she rolls Occult (or Science against Geniuses), not Empathy, at say a -2 penalty for targets unrelated to Princesses (waive the Penalty if the Princess belongs to an appropriate Embassy, or make Embassy membership a prerequisite if you prefer).\end{mybox}

When used against Ephemeral Beings, such as Dreamlanders, Unweaving can be used to undo the effects of Influences and Numina. Unweaving cannot be used to undo Manifestations, it can remove Manifestatinon Conditions if they were created by an appropriate method. For example, Unweaving can remove the Anchor condition if was created with the Charm Resting Place, an Infleunce or a Numina. It could not remove the condition from a natural Anchor such as a ghost's old bones.

\subsubsection*{Fatigue}

 Characters can remain awake for up to 24 hours without too much trouble, but fatigue sets in past that point. At 24 hours, and each 6 hours after that, a character trying to stay awake must roll Stamina + Resolve. If he fails that roll he goes to sleep. If he succeeds, he stays awake, but he takes a cumulative -1 penalty to all his actions, including the roll to stay awake, and unless they get an Exceptional Success they also have to take a Condition. Physically demanding actions can increase a fatigue penalty by -1 to -3. The longest anyone can go without sleep is a number of days equal to the lower of Stamina or Resolve. 
 \end{mybox}

The vision lasts between three and thirty seconds, and appears to the Princess' eyes as if she were at the event in question -- it's up to her to interpret what she sees.

\begin{myindentpar}{10pt}\textbf{Upgrade: Lasting}

\noindent \textbf{Cost:} +1 Wisp

\noindent \textbf{Duration:} 1 scene 

The vision the Princess receives includes the context of the significant event. She sees a full scene of events, to which the key event serves as a climax. The time it takes her to experience this scene is the same as the time it originally took, and while the vision lasts she enters a meditative trance, only vaguely aware of your immediate surroundings; she forgoes Defense until the Charm ends. If something happens near her, the Princess rolls Wits + Composure - 3 to end the Charm prematurely; if she takes damage, the Charm ends immediately.

\textbf{Upgrade: Specific}

The Princess can look for events other than the most significant one. In a repeated use of the Charm, she takes a penalty equal to the number of events she has already seen from the target object to see visions of an event she hasn't seen before (-1 for the second event, -2 for the third, and so on.) Repeating a vision she has seen already is at no penalty, though it may require more successes if enough time has passed by.\end{myindentpar}


\subsubsection*{Genius Loci (Learn \textbullet\textbullet\textbullet, Legno \textbullet\textbullet)} 

\noindent Extended, Manipulation + Occult - Gauntlet Strength (5 minutes/roll, threshold = twice Sanctuary Size)

\noindent \textbf{Cost:} 1 Wisp

\noindent \textbf{Duration:} 1 scene \\

\noindent Places, especially buildings, have their own sort of life; as they age and people care for them, they acquire a distinctive Spirit and a voice that speaks in creaking boards or rustling leaves. With careful attention and a touch of magic, the Princess can rouse a place’s spirit and listen to its voice. When activating the Charm, the Noble meditates inside the place she wants to listen and speak to. 

\begin{myindentpar}{10pt}\emph{Dramatic Failure:} The Princess rouses the place’s spirit, but offends it. Until she leaves it, a curse of ill luck dogs her; all her actions suffer a -1 penalty. If she returns at any time during the next day, she suffers the same penalty until she leaves again. 

\emph{Failure:} The Princess cannot hear the place’s spirit yet. 

\emph{Success:} The Princess makes progress in waking the place’s Spirit. When she reaches the threshold, the Spirit is fully roused and communicates with her in a fashion only she
understands (patterns of creaks, flickering in the lights, etc.) In essence the Princess applies a the Reaching Manifestation Condition onto the Spirit of the local area and temporally grants it a Numina that allows it to hear the and understand the Princess' voice and to send messages which the Princess alone will understand. However magical abilities may allow others to eavesdrop on the conversation and translate. 

\emph{Exceptional Success:} The place’s spirit finds the Princess agreeable. Her she presents a First Impression one level higher than she otherwise would have. \end{myindentpar}

\subsubsection*{Reflected Portents (Learn \textbullet\textbullet\textbullet, Specchio \textbullet\textbullet\textbullet)}

\noindent \textbf{Action:} Instant, Composure + Investigation

\noindent \textbf{Cost:} 1 Wisp, 1 Willpower \\

\noindent How does the Queen of Mirrors know who is the true heir? Simple, she can see the future. Or maybe not, since none of her many True Hairs fulfilled their supposed destiny. Regardless of the truth surrounding the Lightbringer's mysterious Queen, it is a fact that the Invocation of Specchio unlocks powers of precognition beyond the dreams common to all Enlightened.

To perceive the mysteries of time and space the Princess decides which question she wishes to ask, she has to ask about a single person's future. \textquotedblleft Will I be crowned the prom queen?\textquotedblright\, or \textquotedblleft when will I next be attacked is OK?\textquotedblright. The princess could even ask \textquotedblleft who will next attack me\textquotedblright\, because that is part of her personal timeline but she could not ask \textquotedblleft who will be the next Miss America\textquotedblright.

The Princess doesn't have to ask about herself, she could ask who will next attack any specific person. All she needs is a mirror positioned so she can see the subject of her question within. 

There is a modifier to the roll based on it's temporal proximity, because of this the Storyteller should always roll for Reflected Portents. A smart player can learn much from just the modifier.

\begin{mybox} 

\textbf{Temporal Flux}

\begin{itemize}[label={}]

    \item Within a day: 0
    \item Within a week: -1
    \item Within a month: -2
    \item Within a year: -3
    \item Within five years: -4
    \item Within 10 years: -5
    \item Every additional ten years: an additional -1
	
	\end{itemize}
	\end{mybox}

   \begin{myindentpar}{10pt} \emph{Dramatic Failure:} Guided by Fate, or perhaps an unwelcome hint from her own subconscious the Princess sees a horrifying vision. Such as the brutal murder of a close friend. Like all prophecies it's not guaranteed to come true, but she still needs to make a Sensitivity roll.

    \emph{Failure:} The future remains unclear, either nothing happens or the Princess gets a completely accurate vision of a clear blue sky sometime in the future. Or the depths of space, or tv static.

    \emph{Success:} After a few moments the mirror fades to reveal a vision of the future. The player asks one question per Success which the vision will answer. Both sight and sound are provided by the mirror, though both are limited by the quality of the mirror. Anyone looking at the mirror (which doesn't have to be the Princess, if she turns the mirror away after the Charm takes effect) can see the vision. Visions revealed in Reflected Portents are entirely true and accurate, but may nevertheless change for the future remains in flux. Strange beings move outside of Fate, time travellers occasionally change history, even the Princess' vision of the future may introduce new variables into the timeline; perhaps allowing her to escape a horrible fate.

    \emph{Exceptional Success:} Extra Successes are their own reward.\end{myindentpar}

The Princess can only invoke this Charm on the same event once per day. Additionally she cannot learn anything from the past or the present (defined as the current scene). If the Princess asks about a past or present event roll as normal. On a Success reveal that it has already happened but give no further details. If the question has multiple answers; such as asking who will I marry, for someone likely to marry many times, choose the soonest event as defined by the dice pool modifier. If there are multiple events within the same time period favor big and \textquotedblleft noticeable\textquotedblright\, events.

\begin{myindentpar}{10pt}\textbf{Upgrade: Distant (Specchio \textbullet\textbullet\textbullet\textbullet)}

The Princess can prophecies about a distant target by adding Intimacy to the roll. She must name who she is targeting before rolling.

\textbf{Upgrade: What will be? ( Specchio \textbullet\textbullet\textbullet\textbullet\textbullet)}

The Princess no longer needs to name a specific person to scry against. She can instead ask a freeform question like \textquotedblleft where will the next Tainted Place in this town appear?\textquotedblright If more than one person, place or other fits the question then the Charm favors the lowest dice pool modifier from temporal distance. If there are multiple possibilities at the same modifier, the Charm favors big and \textquotedblleft noticeable\textquotedblright\, events. \end{myindentpar}

\subsubsection*{Journey in Reverie (Learn \textbullet\textbullet\textbullet\textbullet, Fuoco \textbullet\textbullet\textbullet)}

\noindent \textbf{Action:} Instant, 

\noindent \textbf{Cost:} 2 Wisps

\noindent \textbf{Dice Pool:} Composure + Empathy

\noindent \textbf{Duration:} Lasting \\

\noindent The Noble dreams of reality, casting her mind forth to walk, invisible and untouchable, in the waking world. The Embassy to the Dreamlands can combine this Charm with Christopher's Parallel Lives, but twisting their privilege in this way is mentally draining and requires one willpower per scene in which the Charm is active.

\begin{myindentpar}{10pt} \emph{Dramatic Failure:} The Noble immediately falls asleep without dreaming, and can’t be wakened by any normal means; magic to wake her up faces a Clash of Wills. The sleep lasts long enough to refresh her Willpower

    \emph{Failure:} The Noble remains awake.

    \emph{Success:} The Noble falls into reverie similar to Dreamlands travel, but rather than pass the threshold of her Crawlspace her dream form remains within the real world. In this state the Noble acts as an ephemeral entity in Twilight Form, with the traits of her Dream Form, and in phase with other psychic projections, Astral beings and Dreamlanders. Thus supernatural powers that influence minds or Dreamlanders in Twilight work on her, but nothing material can touch or perceive her without such a power. The Noble may end the Charm at any time, which returns her to her body and wakes her; until it ends she knows nothing of her body’s condition.
	
	The Noble’s own powers are sharply limited in her Dream Form - unless her target is another ephemeral entity in phase with her, she may use only those Charms that influence minds, or Charms she can cast through a sympathetic connection (her connection to anything she sees is Sensory) and she may not use Charms that require her target to perceive her. While she doesn’t suffer Essence bleed, the Charm expires, waking her, if she can’t stay transformed. Should her Dream Form be destroyed, the Noble wakes with the Soul Shocked Condition.

    \emph{Exceptional Success:}  The Noble finds it easy to sustain her Dream Form outside of her body. She adds +2 to her rolls to stay transformed until the Charm ends.
	
	\textbf{Upgrade: Phantasmal}

    Until the Charm ends, the Noble may spend a Wisp to make her Dream Form visible and audible to material beings for one scene. When she does so, she may use Charms that require others to see or hear her.
	
	\end{myindentpar}
